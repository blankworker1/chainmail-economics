# Chainmail Economics

Oct 4, 2026 · @Carl

Chainmail Economics pictures an economy built from individuals instead of institutions, where people are links in a chainmail of interlocking rings.

It starts from one question: how can a community reduce its dependence on outside companies and governments? Its answer is a structure simple enough for anyone to hold in their head. The basics can be taught in one lesson.

## The model

### The link

Each person is represented by one link: the basic unit of ownership, production, and value. A link holds five attributes in differing amounts: energy, food, land, money, and skills. Nobody holds all five in full, and one is enough to be a valid link. The differences between links are the reason they need each other.

### The five attributes

Each link has a level for each of five attributes. Together they describe what a person holds and can offer.

Each level is a number from 0 to 9, declared by the holder. A card is the five numbers in the order E F L M S, for example 37250.

| Attribute | Letter | What it covers | Everyday examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Energy | E | Power you can produce or store | Solar panels, a battery, firewood |
| Food | F | Food you can grow, raise, preserve, or cook | A vegetable patch, hens, baking, preserves |
| Land | L | Space you own or have the use of | A garden, a roof, a spare room, a workshop |
| Money | M | Sats you hold yourself | Savings, earnings not yet spent |
| Skills | S | What you know how to do and can teach | Repairing, caring, building, cooking, languages |

**Nobody has nothing.** Every person can do something that someone else needs, so everyone has a skills level above zero. One attribute is enough to be a valid link, which means the model is open to everyone.

The levels are a description, not a ranking. A low level shows what a link will look to others for, and a high level shows what it can offer. Levels also change over time: skills are learned from other links, and the other four grow through exchange, saving, and gifts.

### The weave

Each link bonds with four others, as in the four-in-one weave of real chainmail. No link holds more, so there are no hubs. A strong bond comes first and is the condition for joining.

The seed structure is five links: four passing through one. Growth is slow and happens at the edge, one bond at a time. The larger the chainmail, the stronger it is and the more places it offers for new links to join.

### Movement

Value is counted in sats. A transaction can occur between any two links, and the value travels through the bonds between them, like current through metal.

### Heat

The heat map shows two things about every link: what it offers, and how much that offer is wanted where it sits. The map is anonymous. A link on it is only a link.

**Colour shows what a link offers.** Each attribute has its own colour and letter. A link takes the colour of its dominant attribute. A unit takes the colour of the dominant attribute in its combined bars.

**Strength shows how wanted the offer is.** The strength of the colour compares a link's dominant attribute with how much of it the link's bonded neighbours already hold. It uses the neighbours' average level in that attribute, on the 0 to 9 scale, split into three equal bands.

| Strength | Neighbours' average | Shown as | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wanted | Below 3 | Full colour | The neighbours hold little of this attribute |
| Neutral | 3 to below 6 | Normal colour | The neighbours hold a moderate amount |
| Cold | 6 and above | Pale colour, dashed outline | The neighbours already hold plenty |

A link with no bonds has no neighbours to compare with, so it is neutral.

**Hot spots need no marking of their own.** Hot and cold are the same condition at two scales. A unit holding much more of one attribute than it uses is hot in it, and the links carrying that attribute inside it are cold. A hot spot shows as a patch of one colour with faded links inside. A healthy cloth looks mottled.

**The indicator is a signal, not a penalty.** A cold link has three responses within the rules of the game:

- **Move.** Break away and ask again where its colour is scarce.
- **Change.** Levels vary over time, and a second bar can be developed.
- **Wait.** Neighbours bring in arrivals of other colours.

It guides arrivals too. A single link that asks where its colour is rare enters wanted. Heat shifts as the cloth changes, and where a hot spot persists, new links are drawn in.

## The seven aspects

These seven cover what an individual does in the model.

1. **Owning.** Each link owns what it holds: its share of energy, food, land, money, and skills. The owner decides whether each is available or unavailable to others.
2. **Producing.** Producing is applying your skills to what you hold, so that it becomes something another person can use.
3. **Exchanging.** Exchanging is passing value to another link and receiving something in return. Any two links can exchange, and the payment travels through the bonds between them.
4. **Saving.** Saving is holding value at rest until it is needed. A link saves first and spends after. Value held beyond what the link needs is surplus, and it shows as heat.
5. **Giving.** Giving is passing value with nothing asked in return. It is an economic act, and it meets the needs that cannot wait.
6. **No debt.** Everything available in the chain mail is visible, so each link can see what the whole cloth contains. Nothing is promised that doesn't already exist. The model doesn't need debt to work.
7. **Pricing.** A price is whatever two links agree, counted in sats. Offers are public, so anyone can compare them.

## The two attitudes

The model assumes two attitudes of every link. The structure doesn't produce them; each link brings them.

- **Enough.** Each link knows the level at which its needs are met.
- **Low time preference.** Each link is willing to wait, valuing later over now.

Together they explain why the model needs no debt. A link that knows what is enough, and is willing to wait, can save first and spend after.

## How it differs from the traditional model

The traditional model pairs hidden limits with unlimited wants and bridges the gap with debt. The Chainmail model pairs visible limits with enough and bridges the gap with patience and gifts.

|  | Traditional model | Chainmail model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Built from | Institutions | Individuals |
| Understanding | Not needed in order to take part | Explainable in one lesson |
| Connections | Hubs and intermediaries | Four bonds per link |
| Means | Limited, and the whole is hidden from the individual | Bounded by what the mail contains, visible to all |
| Wants | Assumed unlimited | Enough |
| Timing | Have now, pay later | Low time preference: save first, then spend |
| Bridging a gap | Debt, a claim on the future | Exchange of what exists, with gifting for exceptions |
| Growth | Fast, by scale | Slow, one bond at a time |

The Chainmail model pays for these choices with slower growth and less specialisation.

## Three views

Up close, the model shows a single link. Further out, it shows the weave. From a distance it shows a continuous cloth, flexible and whole.

## The game

The model can be played in a classroom with one spring-gate clip ring per participant. Each ring is that person's link. The aim is to maintain and grow the cloth.

### Setup

- Each participant takes one ring and fills in a card: a username, which can be anonymous, and five levels from 0 to 9 for E, F, L, M, and S.
- The facilitator runs the game on one large screen and enters every card and every action. The board shows no names. Usernames appear only in the queue of single links.
- The first link is placed at the centre of the board. Everyone else begins as a single link in the queue.

### Rules

1. Play goes in rounds, with each participant taking a turn.
2. On its turn, a single link picks an open place and asks every link next to it to bond.
3. It enters if at least one accepts, and bonds with each one that accepts.
4. If all refuse, it tries a different place, up to three attempts in the turn. After a third refusal it stays single and asks again next round.
5. Single links may bond with each other, which starts a new seed.
6. A link in the cloth may accept or deny a request at any time, and may later accept one it refused.
7. A link in the cloth may break one bond per turn. A link that breaks its last bond is single again.
8. No link holds more than four bonds.
9. No link leaves the game.

### Accepting

Accepting a bond is close to giving. It costs the link in the cloth nothing, and it gives a newcomer a place. Each bond makes both links, and the cloth they share, stronger. Refusal is always open to a link, and so is accepting later what it refused at first.

### Compatibility

The five levels are advice, not a rule. Two links are compatible where their levels differ, since each then has something the other lacks. The decision to bond belongs only to the holders of the adjacent links, and nothing is refused automatically.

When a link asks at a place with several neighbours, each neighbour decides separately. The new link enters if at least one accepts.

### The unit

A unit is a link together with the neighbours it is bonded to, five links when complete. Its combined profile is the five bars of all its members added together. Every link is the centre of one unit and a member of up to four others, so one arrival changes several units at once.

When a link asks to bond, each neighbour can see what it would bring to their own unit. An arrival always adds to the combined bars, so the advice is about how much is added and where. The only cost of accepting is one of a link's four places. Incomplete units at the edge count in the same way.

Each link has a dominant attribute, the strongest of its five bars. If two bars tie, the holder chooses. A unit has a dominant attribute too, worked out the same way from its combined bars, and a unit whose bars tie has none.

### Shock

From time to time a link is removed at random, by the facilitator or on an automatic timer, and its holder returns as a single link. A shock opens a place inside the cloth, so a later arrival can enter there. Breaking a bond is the slow version of the same event.

### The weakest link

A weakest link is one whose removal would divide the cloth. Arrivals who take the places offering the most bonds remove weakest links as they join.

### What to expect

- **Shape.** The expected outcome is a single cloth, close to circular.
- **Moving.** Any link can break free within four rounds and start again in the next. Staying is a choice.
- **Fragmentation.** Several separate cloths are a sign of instability, not of failure. Whether that is healthy or a natural progression is the question for discussion.
- **Competition.** Competition between links inside the cloth is normal and expected.

## Status

The model is a theoretical visualisation of self-coordinated behaviour.

Bitcoin is its monetary layer because it is the best available tool, and using it requires no knowledge of how payments are routed.

One part is still to come: the extension to community and regional scale.
